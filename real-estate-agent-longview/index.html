What should a real estate agent handle?

An agent should help you make informed decisions from the first conversation through the agreed next steps of a transaction. That can include learning your goals, discussing the property and its likely market, coordinating viewings or inquiries, helping present or review offers, and keeping track of deadlines.

The exact work depends on whether you are buying, selling, renting, or handling a commercial property. Before you commit, ask which tasks the agent will personally handle, what will be handled by other staff, and which matters require a separate professional, such as legal, inspection, lending, or tax advice.

What should a local professional check first?

A useful first conversation is about your plans, not a sales pitch. The agent should understand your preferred timing, property type, budget or expected proceeds, and any practical requirements that could narrow your choices.

  • For a buyer, clarify location, property condition, intended use, financing progress, and which features are essential versus flexible.
  • For a seller, review the property's condition, recent improvements, likely buyer concerns, and what preparation you are willing to do before marketing.
  • For a rental or commercial transaction, discuss the intended use, lease needs, occupancy timing, and any questions that may need review by a lawyer or local authority.
  • For any property, ask how the agent checks available information and explains what still needs independent verification.

Local knowledge is most useful when it is tied to the property in front of you. Ask the agent to explain the basis for recommendations and to point out where records, inspections, or professional advice are still needed.

What can change the cost and timing?

There is no single fee that applies to every real estate service. The amount and method of compensation depend on the service requested and the written agreement. Ask how the fee is calculated, when it is due, what work it covers, and whether any other charges may apply. Do not rely on assumptions based on a past transaction or another provider's terms.

  • The property type and transaction, including whether the work involves a purchase, sale, rental, or commercial property.
  • The scope of help, such as marketing, showings, tenant communication, offer support, or coordination with other parties.
  • Property condition, missing information, access for viewings, and the need for inspections or additional records.
  • How quickly the parties respond, whether negotiations are straightforward, and whether financing or other contingencies affect the schedule.

Timing is not fully controlled by an agent. A slow document review, a change in financing, inspection findings, or delays from another party can shift a schedule. Ask for the next expected milestones and how you will be updated if dates change.

What local rules and property details need attention?

Before relying on a property's intended use, check the applicable records and requirements for that specific address or parcel. Zoning, permits, property disclosures, lease terms, and other requirements can vary by property and transaction. An agent can help identify questions to pursue, but should not be treated as a substitute for legal advice or confirmation from the relevant local authority.

If a commercial property or rental is involved, describe the planned use and timing early. Ask what information is available, what remains unverified, and which questions should go to an attorney, inspector, lender, or public office before you make a commitment.

What mistakes should you avoid?

  • Choosing based only on a review score without checking whether the provider handles your type of transaction.
  • Signing before you understand the agreement's length, scope, compensation, cancellation terms, and any exclusivity requirements.
  • Assuming a verbal promise is included when it is not written into the service agreement.
  • Treating marketing descriptions or informal estimates as verified facts about condition, boundaries, permitted use, or value.
  • Waiting until a deadline is close to raise concerns about financing, access, inspections, or documents.

Reviews can help you spot patterns in customer experiences, but one rating cannot show how an agent will handle your particular needs. Ask for a clear explanation and take time to read the agreement before you sign.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Have you handled transactions like mine, and what parts of this property or transaction need extra attention?
  • Who will be my main contact, and who steps in if that person is unavailable?
  • What services are included in your proposed agreement, and what falls outside its scope?
  • How is your compensation set, when is it due, and could other charges apply?
  • How will you share updates, and how quickly should I expect a response?
  • What could delay this transaction, and which dates or decisions should I plan around?
  • What agreement am I being asked to sign, and how do its term, cancellation, and exclusivity provisions work?
