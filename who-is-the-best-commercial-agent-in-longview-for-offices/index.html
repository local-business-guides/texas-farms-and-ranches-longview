What should an office commercial agent handle?

An office commercial agent can help a business find space to lease or buy, or help a property owner market space and consider offers. The work should start with the client's goals: location, size, access, budget, timing, and the terms that matter most.

A useful service may include identifying suitable properties, arranging tours, comparing options, communicating offers, and helping track the transaction through its next steps. Ask what is included in the agent's work and what requires a lawyer, inspector, lender, contractor, or other specialist. How can you compare office properties fairly?

Start with a written list of must-haves and preferences. A business that needs customer access may rank parking and visibility differently from a team that mainly needs private work areas. Comparing spaces against the same list helps keep a polished tour from outweighing practical needs.

  • Usable layout, room configuration, and the amount of space the business actually needs.
  • Access, parking, visibility, and convenience for staff, customers, or deliveries.
  • Lease or purchase terms, including responsibilities for maintenance and other property costs.
  • Condition of the space and any work needed before opening or moving in.
  • Timing, including availability, approvals, build-out, and the planned move date.

Ask the agent to show how each option meets your priorities and where the compromises are. For a lease, compare the full obligations and not only the advertised rent; have a qualified attorney review legal terms before you sign.

What changes the cost and timing?

There is no single fee or schedule for every office transaction. Cost can depend on whether you are leasing, buying, selling, or marketing a property, how much work the assignment requires, and the written agreement between you and the provider. Ask how compensation is calculated, who pays it, when it is due, and whether any services have separate charges.

Timing can change with property availability, the number of spaces to review, negotiations, financing, inspections, approvals, and any repairs or build-out. A transaction can also take longer if the parties need to settle questions about permitted use, maintenance duties, or lease terms. Ask for a realistic sequence of steps and what could delay each one rather than relying on a promised closing or move date.

What local details should you verify?

Before committing to an office, verify that the proposed use is allowed for that specific property and check any applicable zoning, occupancy, signage, parking, and building requirements with the relevant local authority. Requirements can vary by location and by the way a space will be used, so do not assume that a previous tenant's setup will suit your business.

Also confirm who is responsible for utilities, repairs, common areas, insurance, and any work needed before occupancy. An agent can help identify questions and coordinate communication, but legal, construction, code, and tax advice should come from the appropriate qualified professional.

What mistakes should you avoid?

  • Choosing based only on a review score or a quick recommendation without checking the agent's relevant commercial experience.
  • Signing a representation or listing agreement before understanding its duration, scope, compensation, and cancellation terms.
  • Comparing spaces by advertised rent alone instead of reviewing the full lease or ownership costs.
  • Treating a tour or verbal explanation as confirmation that the property meets every legal, building, or operational requirement.
  • Waiting until the last minute to identify move-in needs, repairs, approvals, or a lease start date.

A short written summary of your requirements and the terms you understand can prevent avoidable confusion. If a point affects your legal obligations or total cost, get it clarified in writing and have the right specialist review it.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What commercial office transactions have you handled that are similar to mine?
  • Who will be my main contact, and who will cover the work if that person is unavailable?
  • What services are included, and what would be outside the agreed scope?
  • How is your compensation calculated, who pays it, and when?
  • How will you help me compare spaces or offers beyond the asking price?
  • What information do you need from me to begin, and what is a realistic timeline?
  • How will you check property-use questions and coordinate with local authorities or other specialists?
  • What agreement would I sign, how long would it last, and how can either party end it?

Clear answers should make it easier to compare providers. If an answer is vague, ask for a specific example or have the provider put the relevant service or fee term in writing.
