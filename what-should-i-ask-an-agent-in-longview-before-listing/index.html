What should a listing service cover?

A listing agent should help you set a pricing strategy, prepare the property for the market, coordinate listing details, and manage offers and negotiations. Ask who will handle each part, how you’ll receive updates, and what happens after an offer comes in. The answers should be specific to your property, not just a standard sales pitch.

For a home, the discussion may focus on condition, presentation, and comparable properties. Land or commercial property can call for different information, such as access, boundaries, permitted uses, leases, or income details. Ask the agent to explain which items apply to your property and who is responsible for checking them.

What should a local professional check before recommending a plan?

A careful agent should look at the property itself and the details that could affect a buyer’s decision. Ask how they’ll compare your property with recent sales and current competition, and whether those comparisons are genuinely similar in location, size, use, and condition. For acreage or commercial property, ask how the agent will account for differences that make a simple price-per-acre or price-per-square-foot comparison misleading.

The agent should also ask what you know about the property and point out information that may need confirmation. Depending on the property, that can include survey or boundary information, access, utilities, leases, restrictions, or required disclosures. Ask what the agent can verify, what needs another professional’s help, and what should be addressed before the listing goes live.

What drives the cost of listing with an agent?

The cost depends on the agreement and the work involved, so ask for the proposed compensation and any other charges in writing. The property type, marketing plan, level of service, and any additional photography, preparation, or coordination can affect what is included. Don’t assume a particular task is covered just because it is commonly offered.

Ask whether compensation is negotiable, when it is earned, and whether any costs are due if the property does not sell. Have the agent explain cancellation terms, the listing period, and any services billed separately. Compare the full written terms and the work promised, rather than choosing from a headline fee alone.

What can change the listing timeline?

Timing can depend on how ready the property is, how quickly you can provide records, and whether any repairs or professional checks are needed. Marketing plans and buyer activity also affect how long a property takes to sell, and an agent can’t guarantee a sale date. Ask for a proposed schedule with the steps that are within your control clearly separated from market-dependent steps.

For property with acreage, leases, commercial use, or other details that need confirming, allow time to gather and review the relevant documents. Ask which items should be ready before signing and which can be completed while the listing is being prepared. If the schedule matters because of a move, purchase, or other commitment, explain that early.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What recent sales and current listings are most comparable to my property, and why?
  • How will you recommend a list price, and what information could change that recommendation?
  • What work will you do before the listing goes live, and what will I need to arrange?
  • Who will be my main contact, and how often will you update me?
  • How will you market this property to likely buyers, and what materials or services are included?
  • What is your proposed compensation, what other costs may apply, and when are they due?
  • What are the listing term, cancellation conditions, and any obligations if the property does not sell?
  • What documents or property details should I gather, and which items should I verify with another professional?

What mistakes should you avoid?

  • Hiring based only on the highest suggested list price. Ask what comparable evidence supports it and how the plan would change if buyers don’t respond.
  • Signing before you understand the agreement. Review the term, compensation, included services, cancellation language, and any additional charges.
  • Assuming all agents handle land, commercial property, and homes the same way. Ask about experience relevant to your property and who will manage specialized questions.
  • Leaving known property details until the last minute. Share what you know about boundaries, access, leases, condition, and restrictions, then ask what needs confirmation.
  • Relying on a verbal promise about marketing or communication. Ask for the plan and expectations in writing so you can compare providers fairly.

When should you call a listing professional?

Talk with an agent before you set a price, commit to major preparation work, or make a firm move based on an expected sale date. A conversation can help identify records to gather and questions that need a specialist, such as legal, survey, inspection, or tax advice. The agent should be clear about where their role ends and another professional’s begins.

What’s the next step?

Choose two or three providers to interview and ask each the same core questions. Bring the property details you have, request a written plan and agreement, and compare the explanation, communication, and services alongside the proposed terms.
