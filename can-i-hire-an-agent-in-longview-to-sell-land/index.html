What should an agent handle when selling land?

Land sales take more than putting a property online. An agent can help you set a marketing plan, prepare property information, arrange showings or site visits, communicate with buyers, and work through an offer and the steps toward closing. The exact services depend on the listing agreement, so ask what is included before you hire.

A useful plan should account for what makes your parcel different from a house or commercial building. Buyers may want details about access, utilities, boundaries, land use, timber, minerals, or any improvements. An agent should help organize available information and flag questions that need an answer from a surveyor, attorney, title company, or other qualified professional.

What will a Longview-area professional check?

Before recommending a listing strategy, a professional should get a clear picture of the property and your goals. That usually starts with the deed or other ownership records, parcel information, acreage, location, and any known restrictions or agreements. If land crosses a county line or records use different parcel descriptions, confirm the details with the appropriate county offices and title professionals.

They may also ask about road frontage and legal access, utilities, drainage, current use, easements, surveys, and whether any minerals or timber rights are included in the sale. City limits, an extraterritorial jurisdiction, county requirements, septic considerations, and flood information can matter too. The agent can help identify what needs checking, but should not present an unverified assumption as a confirmed fact.

Bring any surveys, deeds, tax records, leases, utility information, and prior studies you have. If something is missing, ask which records the agent can help locate and which items you should verify through a specialist.

What affects the cost of hiring an agent?

There is no single fee that applies to every land sale. The amount and structure are set through the agreement between the owner and the brokerage, and can depend on the services requested, the property, and how the sale is handled. Discuss the proposed fee and any other costs before signing rather than relying on a general estimate.

Ask whether the agreement covers photography, mapping, signs, online promotion, buyer follow-up, offer discussions, and coordination with the closing professionals. Some property may need additional work, such as a current survey, access improvements, cleanup, or specialist reports. Those expenses are separate questions to discuss, and you should confirm who authorizes and pays for each one.

Compare the full written terms, including the listing period, how the agreement can end, and what happens if a buyer is already known to you. A lower fee may come with fewer services, while a broader plan may include more hands-on support. Ask for the difference in writing.

What can change the time it takes to sell?

Timing depends on the land, the asking price, buyer demand, and how ready the property information is. A parcel with unclear boundaries, access questions, missing documents, or unresolved ownership details may take longer to prepare and move through a sale. Offers can also take time to compare and negotiate.

Ask the agent how the proposed price will be supported, what buyer group the marketing is meant to reach, and how often you will get updates. A thoughtful plan can set expectations, but no agent can guarantee a sale date. If a buyer needs financing, inspections, surveys, or other contingencies, those can affect the closing schedule as well.

What mistakes should landowners avoid?

A few basic checks can prevent confusion later. Before listing, be candid about anything you know that could affect a buyer's use or understanding of the land.

  • Do not assume the acreage, boundary, access, or utility details are correct without checking the available records.
  • Do not advertise mineral rights, timber, water access, or development potential as included or confirmed unless you have verified the facts.
  • Do not sign an agreement without understanding its services, fee terms, length, and cancellation process.
  • Do not choose a provider based only on a suggested asking price. Ask how the recommendation was developed and what comparable information supports it.
  • Do not treat an agent's guidance as legal, tax, engineering, or surveying advice. Bring those questions to the appropriate licensed professional.

Questions to Ask Before Hiring an Agent

  • How much recent experience do you have selling land similar to mine in or near Longview?
  • Who will be my main contact, and who handles inquiries and showings?
  • What is included in your proposed listing service, and what costs extra?
  • How will you support the asking-price recommendation?
  • How will you market the parcel and reach buyers who may be interested in this kind of land?
  • What property records or specialist reports do you recommend I gather before listing?
  • How often will you update me, and what happens if the listing agreement needs to end early?
  • What do you see as the biggest questions a buyer may raise about this property?

Listen for specific answers tied to your parcel, not broad promises. A good fit should be willing to explain what the agent knows, what still needs verification, and which tasks fall outside the agent's role.

When should you call a professional?

Talk with an agent before setting a public asking price or agreeing to terms with a potential buyer, especially if you are unsure how to describe access, boundaries, or included rights. A local professional can help you plan the sale and identify information buyers are likely to request. If ownership, inheritance, mineral rights, or legal access is disputed or unclear, get advice from an appropriate attorney or title professional too.
